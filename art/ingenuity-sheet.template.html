Ingenuity

The Mars helicopter, drawn chunky to match Sojourner: big shapes, one outline, two tones per part, huge eyes on its body. Two rotors on a mast with the solar panel on top, a small box body and four long legs. All art is drawn in code at 40 × 40 pixels and shown enlarged.

Animation
On the board

Takes off from its airfield, flies over a rock, turns to point its camera at the striped rock and takes a photo, then lands on a new airfield. The shadow stays on the ground and drifts down-right as it climbs. The ring shows its front.

Every 45°

Eight headings for the board, rotors spinning. Up shows its back (battery hatch), down shows its face. The three left-facing views are the right-facing ones flipped.

Every frame

Rotors still on the ground, four spin frames in the air. The two rotors turn opposite ways, so their blades cross.

The ground it reads

Plain sand is fine to fly over but not to land on. On the board nothing is highlighted; the child learns these on the briefing card.

Four real flights

Each puzzle is a kind of flight Ingenuity really flew, and each adds one new idea. Best moves rise 5, 7, 11, 14. Shown with Ingenuity at its start, facing its start direction.

Size line-up

The real helicopter is about as tall as Curiosity's wheel is wide. It is drawn larger here so its face reads.

Real hardware kept

  • Two stacked rotors that spin opposite ways, about 2,400 rpm, to lift it in Mars's thin air.
  • A solar panel on top of the mast that charges its batteries.
  • A small box body (1.8 kg in all) on four legs.
  • Two cameras under the body: a colour camera tilted toward the horizon (the "nose" here) and a black-and-white navigation camera looking straight down at the ground.
  • A damaged blade tip (one frame), for the story of its last flight. [source: how the damage looked]

Style rules

  • One dark outline, two tones per part, no fiddly detail.
  • Eyes on the body, never on rotors or legs.
  • Every view fills the same 36 × 38 box, so it never changes size when it turns.
  • Spinning rotors show as a pale blur line plus the blades' current length.
  • No glow. Pink marks the rotor hubs and feet; the tool grey from Sojourner is reused for the camera.
